What is recoverable that used to be lost — the forgetfulness axis

Session 20261112_172849_f7171a9f has 2328 turns summarised away yet kept verbatim. The archived pointer region spans ids 19235–26333 (7099 ids). 5 of 70 fires pointed into a block of 100+ ids — material the live context had already summarised away. Before recall the compression boundary was destructive; now it is a cache miss.

Is the same ground being covered twice? — the rework axis

Intent is not visible in a log, so this axis uses two measured proxies: 29 distinct topics matched in two or more separate fires (20261112_172849_f7171a9f×43, actions×43, active×43, actual×30, access×17, added×14), and 6 cross-session pointers — work this session never had in context at all, so no amount of summarising could have preserved it. Full read-back telemetry: n/a - not measured. The honest claim is therefore “rework was made avoidable”, not “N reruns were avoided”.

Sensitivity: halve the rework assumption to 1500 tokens and break-even rises to 6.41%; double it to 6000 and break-even falls to 1.6%. The verdict moves with the assumption, which is why the assumption is printed next to it.

What would flip the verdict

  • Read-back below 20% of fires → pointers are being paid for and ignored; fix the pointer format, not the store.
  • Low-confidence share rising while strict hits do not → retrieval is regressing; retune the terms and gates.
  • Recurring topics falling toward the break-even rate → recall is buying almost nothing; the re-investigation cost assumption is too generous for this workload.
